The Literature Paradox: Speed vs. Deep Learning

Individual

Structuring vs. Problematizing

Brian Reiser (2004)
  • GenAI easily structures tasks to eliminate short-term struggle and speed up completion.
  • True learning requires problematizing—deliberately introducing complexity so students exercise critical judgment rather than passive tool consumption.
⚠️ Warning: Fluent artifacts do not equate to deep comprehension.
Group

Transactive Memory Systems

Daniel Wegner (1987) · Group Mind & Transactive Memory
  • High-performing collaborative teams succeed because members build a shared mental model of each other's specialized expertise.
  • When AI mediates peer reviews and task allocation, students query AI agents in isolated silos.
⚠️ Warning: Individual-to-AI interaction erodes distributed team awareness.
Relational

AI-Mediated Communication

Hancock, Naaman & Levy (2020)
  • AI is never an objective, neutral pipe. When AI polishes critiques, it alters interpersonal perception.
  • AI-smoothed feedback (e.g., AI code review and approval) creates an illusion of frictionless agreement while masking genuine disagreements.
⚠️ Warning: Erode interpersonal trust and authentic debate needed for team safety.

“Not Less Friction. The Right Friction.”

The Silicon Valley Narrative: Frictionless Automation

  • Goal: Eliminate all waiting, struggle, and effort from knowledge work.
  • Passive Consumption: Immediate polished output bypasses critical problem framing.
  • The Illusion of Mastery: Students feel competent while mental models remain fragile.
  • Result: High execution speed, but zero transfer, erosion of ownership, and model dependence.

Our Human-Centered Agenda: Productive Friction

  • Goal: Intentionally design task-relevant struggle where human cognition matters most.
  • Active Interrogation: Deliberate choice points force learners to justify trade-offs and evaluate models.
  • Scaffolded Challenge: Short-term difficulty creates durable conceptual understanding.
  • Result: Genuine human agency, deep reflective thinking, and preserved authorship.
Core Research Thesis · ACM AI Summit 2026

“AI is often most valuable not when it removes all friction, but when it introduces productive friction.”

Productive friction: Task-relevant pauses, choices, disagreements, and boundaries that require thinkers to exercise judgment, ownership, and responsibility.

The Iceberg Model: Designing for the Invisible Work

Dots On the Iceberg · Individual

Cognitive Friction

Individual critical judgment, interrogation & verification

  • Questioning assumptions Interrogating model premises, framing inquiries, and probing edge cases before accepting answers.
  • Comparing alternative model outputs Evaluating behavioral variance across models rather than relying passively on a single prompt.
  • Debugging subtle hallucinations Spotting latent logic bugs, factual inaccuracies, and unverified statistical claims.
  • Exercising individual critical judgment Cultivating the independent intellectual muscle to reason, disagree, and decide standards.

Relational Friction

Interpersonal dialogue, peer review & transactive memory

  • Explaining decisions to peers Articulating design rationale, code choices, and theoretical trade-offs to teammates.
  • Negotiating genuine disagreements Reconciling differing perspectives and wrestling through conflicts without an AI referee.
  • Reviewing teammate code Auditing peers' human-AI workflows, identifying flaws, and mutual peer mentoring.
  • Building shared transactive memory & trust Constructing collective team intelligence—knowing who knows what and how peers think.

ACM CHI 2024 · Best Paper Honorable Mention ↗

CoQuest: Temporal Friction

Interaction Design

Iterative research-question branching with observable processing delays and active waiting moments. Treats LLMs as supports for literature-grounded ideation rather than instant streaming.

Preserved Capacity

Reflection, control, and felt agency. A small temporal inefficiency creates cognitive space for reflection, correlating with a significantly stronger sense of authorial control.

ACM DL Official ACM Publication
doi:10.1145/3613904.3642698 ↗

Yiren Liu, Si Chen, Haocong Cheng, Mengxia Yu, Xiao Ran, Andrew Mo, Yiliu Tang, Yun Huang. 2024. CoQuest: Exploring Research Question Co-Creation with an LLM-based Agent. In Proceedings of the 2024 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems (CHI '24).

Classroom Parallel: Requiring students to write down their own predictions and architectural sketches before prompting an AI copilot.

ACM DIS 2025 ↗

PersonaFlow: Structural Friction

Interaction Design

Users choose and customize expert personas before receiving critique rather than one monolithic authority. Extends single-agent support to inspectable, multi-perspective ideation.

Preserved Capacity

Ownership, perspective-taking, recall. Persona customization increases users' sense of control and recall of generated ideas, without raising cognitive load.

ACM DL Official ACM Publication
doi:10.1145/3715336.3735789 ↗

Classroom Parallel: Requiring multi-agent adversarial code audits and diverse stakeholder review panels during sprint evaluations.

ACM CHI 2026 · Best Paper Honorable Mention ↗

Perspectra: Interactional Friction

Interaction Design

Agents are designed to show users papers that make contrary findings or complementary perspectives to existing work, scaffolding threaded multi-agent deliberation where users visualize tensions and revise proposals.

Preserved Capacity

Critical thinking, revision, argument integration. More critical-thinking behavior and proposal revision than a group-chat baseline—AI responses become positions to question, not material to copy.
